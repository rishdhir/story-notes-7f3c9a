Design thesis

The work is the centre of attention.

This document records Rish’s exploration of storytelling in the AI age. The writing explains the idea, its context and the production experiment in plain language; it does not present a product for sale. The defining visual is a sequence of portrait-format moments from the same character’s life: the format and the continuity are visible before they are explained.

Its structure draws on Rish’s established media-led pipeline documents. Its interface applies Apple’s principles of clear hierarchy, familiarity and accessibility, with Emil Kowalski’s guidance on brief, purposeful interaction. Liquid Glass belongs to the floating controls, while the content itself stays solid and legible.

The document’s polished presentation and the films’ candid phone aesthetic serve different purposes. The website frames the work; it does not redefine Erica’s photographic look.

Visual language

Quiet surroundings. Recognisable work.

Large, closely spaced headings introduce each idea. Body copy uses a comfortable measure and a restrained hierarchy. Graphite foundations, midnight-blue process sections, muted-plum character sections, warm charcoal story sections and dark-teal automation sections echo the outdoor wardrobe and London setting without competing with the source imagery.

Typography and spacing

  • Inter, self-hosted, with system-sans fallbacks. No external font request is needed at reading time.
  • A fluid display scale, 16px base text and 14–17px supporting copy. Metadata is smaller and never carries the main argument.
  • Body text stays near a readable line length; generous spacing separates ideas rather than adding decorative containers.
  • Layout adapts to available width. Portrait video preserves its original aspect ratio. Sheet previews open in a full-view dialog.

“Story Systems” is an editorial working label for this document, not a newly approved public series title. There is no BBC, Apple or provider branding implied.

Material

A functional layer of glass.

The floating navigation, segmented arc selector, sheet inspection and playback controls use the requested Liquid Glass implementation. Chromium receives SVG displacement at the rim; Safari and Firefox use its frosted-blur fallback. The effect never carries essential meaning.

  • Use noticeable rim refraction, a fine luminous edge and a translucent smoky fill, with legible monochrome labels.
  • Keep glass on a few navigation or control surfaces. Do not place the main prose or every media card on glass.
  • Respect increased contrast, reduced transparency and reduced motion. Opaque fallback surfaces preserve readable controls.
  • Keep glass surfaces modest in size to avoid unnecessary rendering work.

Interaction

Movement should explain a response.

Use 160–250ms transitions for small changes, with a fast-starting ease-out curve. Press feedback is slight. Hover effects apply only to devices that actually hover. Keyboard tab navigation is immediate.

The workflow explorer and three-part sixty-day arc reveal one part at a time. Image dialogs have an explicit close action, Escape dismissal and focus return. All motion is optional; content is never held behind an entrance animation.

Media and accessibility

Real evidence, carefully presented.

Only the selected approved rebuilt episodes and current approved reference sheets are used. Video originals are copied byte-for-byte. Preview posters are extracted from those files; no replacement scene is invented to make the document look better.

  • Videos use preload="none", show a local poster and start only on request. Offscreen and background-tab videos pause.
  • Large source sheets load lazily. Their captions identify the character or location rather than claiming a new approval.
  • Colour is accompanied by text labels. Controls have visible focus states and useful accessible names.
  • The exact scripts provide text alternatives to the filmed story. Generated on-screen caption errors remain disclosed.
  • Print styles provide a reading copy. The interactive web document remains the primary format for media and exploration.

Design references

The sources behind the choices.

  • Emil Kowalski — Design Engineering: intentional motion, responsive controls, reduced-motion support and interface restraint.
  • Apple Design Skill: hierarchy, accessibility, adaptable layout and separation of content from the functional glass layer.
  • Apple Human Interface Guidelines: the underlying principles, interpreted for an editorial website rather than copied as native-app conventions.
  • Liquid Glass by deepika-builds: the bundled refraction implementation and browser fallback. MIT licence.
  • Inter: the self-hosted typeface. Font licence.
  • Rish’s local BBC pipeline R&D template: precedent for media-led explanation, visual process and reusable prompt sections. This edition uses its editorial principles with a distinct identity.
